What is Pilot

Pilot is a Property Operations Technology Company.

We build the digital infrastructure that powers modern property operations.

Our technology helps real estate developers, property managers, and facility management companies operate their properties with greater visibility, control, and confidence.

From community management and facility operations to resident experiences and operational intelligence, Pilot transforms fragmented processes into one connected ecosystem.

We don't simply build software. We build the operating foundation that helps properties perform better every day.

005
The Ecosystem

One platform, every party

Property operations are not a single company's problem. They are a chain of organisations that must agree with each other continuously. Pilot is the layer they all operate on.

Real Estate DevelopersHandover, community setup, portfolio performance across projects.

Why this matters to the brand

Pilot is never presented as a product for one role. Every application in this book must read credibly to a developer's board, an FM director, an operations supervisor, and a resident on their phone — simultaneously.

Brand Story

Every property is designed to stand for decades.

But its true success is never determined on the day it is built.

It is determined every single day after.

  • How quickly maintenance issues are resolved.
  • How smoothly residents interact with management.
  • How confidently operators make decisions.
  • How efficiently teams work together.
  • How consistently great experiences are delivered.

For years, the real estate industry invested heavily in designing and constructing exceptional properties, while the operational journey that followed remained fragmented — spread across disconnected systems, spreadsheets, phone calls, emails, and manual processes.

010
Brand Story — continued

We believed there had to be a better way.

Not another software product.

Not another isolated application.

But a smarter operational foundation that connects people, processes, and technology into one seamless experience.

That belief became Pilot.

Pilot was created to help real estate companies move beyond operational complexity and toward operational confidence.

Whether an organization is managing its very first development or thousands of properties across multiple cities, Pilot grows with them — providing the visibility, control, and intelligence needed to operate at every stage of their journey.

Today, Pilot is building more than software. We are building the digital infrastructure for the future of property operations.

Because great properties are not defined only by how they are built. They are defined by how they are operated.

014
Voice & Tone

How Pilot speaks

Pilot writes the way a good instrument reads: short, exact, and free of interpretation. If a sentence could be misread by a supervisor at 11pm during an escalation, it is the wrong sentence.

We write

"1,284 units under management. 19 open work orders."

We don't write

"Your portfolio is looking great — but a few things need love!"

We write

"Maintenance request assigned. SLA expires in 4 hours."

We don't write

"Don't worry! We're on it — hang tight 🚀"

We write

"Service charge invoice issued. Awaiting payment."

We don't write

"Awesome! We're almost there — just one more step!"

  • Precise, not clever. A number is more persuasive than an adjective. Use it.
  • Calm, not urgent. The product creates certainty; the language should sound like it.
  • Plain, not corporate. No "leverage", "synergy", "solutions". Say what the thing does.
  • Never exclamatory. Pilot does not celebrate its own features.

016
Identity — Primary Asset

The wordmark

A soft-geometric monoline construction with generous, even tracking. It is a single locked artwork — never re-typed, never re-spaced, never rebuilt from a font.

Primary lockup — Ink on neutral
  • The wordmark is the default. Use it wherever the brand has room to be stated in full.
  • Letter spacing, weight and proportion are fixed. Do not attempt to reproduce it typographically.
  • When it stands alone in a frame, position it optically centred — not mathematically centred.
017
Identity — The Mark

The P is a switch

The counter of the P is not empty space. It is a toggle — a rounded track with a knob resting inside it, the universal symbol for a system that can be switched on, tuned and controlled.

This is the brand idea compressed into one letter. Pilot is the control layer for property operations, and the logo says so before a single word of copy is read.

Why it matters

Most property technology marks borrow a building, a roof, a key or a pin. Those describe the asset. The toggle describes the operation — and Pilot is infrastructure for the operation, not a picture of the property.

  • The knob's size and position within the track are fixed. Never re-centre, resize or animate it in the static logo.
  • The mark may stand alone only where the wordmark is already established, or where space genuinely forbids it — app icons, favicons, avatars, embroidery.
  • Never place the standalone mark beside the wordmark. The word already contains it.
018
Identity — Construction

Built on a unit grid

The wordmark is drawn on a fixed modular grid. Stroke weight, counter radius, terminal treatment and inter-letter spacing are all derived from a single unit — which is why the logo cannot be rebuilt by eye.

Wordmark on construction grid — one square equals one unit
StrokeA single constant weight across every letter. No optical thinning, no contrast.
TerminalsFully rounded, matching the toggle knob. One radius governs the entire alphabet.
TrackingEven and generous. The word breathes; it is never tightened to fit a space.
019
Identity — Clear Space & Scale

Clear space

Minimum clear space on all four sides equals X, where X is the cap height of the wordmark. Nothing enters this zone — no type, no rules, no image edges, no partner logos.

XXXX

Minimum sizes

Below these sizes the toggle inside the P stops resolving and the mark loses its meaning. Switch to the standalone mark rather than shrinking the wordmark further.

Comfortable — 180 px +
Minimum digital — 96 px
Mark minimum — 24 px

Print equivalents

Wordmark minimum width 25 mm. Mark minimum width 7 mm. For embroidery, etching or debossing, add 15% to both.

025
System — Graphic Device

The track

The toggle inside the P extends into a supporting device: a rounded track with a single knob. It carries the brand across formats without repeating the logo, and it earns its place because it means something — position indicates state.

Track device — rhythm and state, used as pattern, divider or accent
  • Use the track as a section divider, a page footer rule, a loading state, or a large-format pattern.
  • The knob is always a perfect circle at 70% of the track height, never an ellipse.
  • The device never appears in the same eye-line as the logo. It supports the identity; it does not compete with it.

Governance

Four things this book cannot settle

The system above is complete. These items sit outside what a document can resolve, and they are listed here so they are not quietly forgotten.

  • A vector master is required. Every logo in this book is a raster image derived from the supplied artwork. Before any print, signage, embroidery or favicon production, the wordmark and mark must be redrawn as clean vector outlines with the toggle geometry mathematically defined. Raster will fail at billboard scale and at 16 px.
  • These mockups are rendered, not photographed. Every application in Chapter Four is drawn in code to show placement, proportion and hierarchy correctly. For a sales deck or website, they should be reproduced as photographic mockups using real product photography — the rules here are what the photographer and retoucher work to.
  • Trademark clearance is outstanding. "Pilot" is a common word and a registered mark in multiple classes. Clearance in every target jurisdiction should be run by counsel before launch spend, not after. The toggle-in-P device should be searched separately as a figurative mark.
  • Arabic is a separate commission. This book covers the Latin lockup only, as requested. For a regional launch, a matched Arabic wordmark must be drawn with the same monoline logic and terminal treatment — never typed in an existing font — and given its own section in Volume Two.

Open decision — flagged for your veto

Signal green (#00B383) is a recommendation, not a locked choice. Property technology is saturated with blue, and green reads as "system active" rather than "financial dashboard". Changing it affects one value and nothing else in the system depends on it.
